Bad Design

https://www.arngren.net/

This website is an example of poor design. The reason KARZ Inc. unanimously agrees that this website is an example of poor design is that this website violates 3 of the principles of Proper User Interface design as described in our textbook. Firstly, it violates the first rule, which is consistency, by having different layouts and configurations on different pages of the website. When a link is clicked, the new page does not resemble the previous one, forcing the user to adapt to a new way of navigation. The ease of navigation principle is also violated because links are placed all over the website, on all pages, and in every space. There is virtually no cohesion with where links are placed, and there are no back buttons to go back to previous pages after clicking one of the links. Lastly, the website violates the Universal Usability and Adaptability principle. This is violated numerous times and in various ways. The web page does not shrink or stretch based on display size (even on a regular 11-inch display, the website is not fitted and requires left-to-right scrollability just to access different parts and buttons of the website). The site is very busy, with images, links, and text tossed everywhere across the display. Text overlaps with images,s and images overlap on text,t, and text is not fitted and chopped off in new lines. There is no alt text on images for the visually impaired, and overall, our team agrees that this website is terrible. (Also, it sometimes cannot be accessed using Mac Wi Fi)

Good Design

https://www.mcmaster.ca/

The McMaster website is a good example of a properly designed webpage. Our team agrees that this website is cohesive and is well designed according to the 4 principles of Proper User Interface Design described in the textbook. Firstly he website is fairly consistent across all pages on the website. There is a nav bar at the top of the webpage that stays there no matter what page you are on. Each page follows a similar pattern, where information regarding specific things is broken up into boxes based on categories, and the pages all follow a similar design, making it easy to get around. The second principle, ease of navigation, is well implemented. The nav bar at the top allows you to navigate to other sections of the website without having to go back to previous pages. Wherever you are on the website, you can easily navigate to a different desired page in seconds. Lastly, the third principle of universal usability and adaptability is also very well followed. The webpage shrinks and expands based on display size, and buttons are also reoriented based on the size as well. The website is neither cluttered nor too empty and has a great mix of visuals as well as text to display information. The colour scheme is consistent between all pages, following the patented burgundy and yellow McMaster colours, which look amazing on the white general background of the website. Upon navigating to various pages, no errors were encountered, which proves that this website is both aesthetically pleasing and easy to navigate, and properly implemented to ensure a positive user experience.
